Sunny & Misty Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 6, 2026

Summary

Sunny & Misty is a weather app made by FangJet LLC ("we", "our", or "us") for iPhone, iPad and Android. It needs no account, shows no ads and does not track you across other apps or websites. We do not sell or rent your information.

To show you the weather, the app sends the location you ask about to weather services, and to name your current location it asks your phone's own place lookup (Apple's or Google's). It also sends anonymous usage statistics, which you can turn off. This page explains exactly what is sent, to whom, and why.

Information kept on your device

  • Saved cities and settings: the cities you add, your units choice and your other settings are stored only on your device.
  • Cached weather: the latest forecast for each city is stored on your device so the app opens instantly and works offline.
  • Notifications: the morning briefing and the walk-time nudge are planned and scheduled on your device.

On Android, the system's Auto Backup may copy the app's settings and saved cities to your own Google account, as it does for most apps. You can turn this off in your device's backup settings. Deleting the app removes everything stored on the device.

Location

Location access is optional. If you allow it, the app reads your device's location to show the weather where you are. Without it, you can add any city by name.

To fetch a forecast, the app sends the coordinates of the city you are viewing (your current location, if you chose it) to the weather services listed below. City names you type are sent to OpenWeather to find matching places. We do not run servers of our own, so your location is never sent to or stored by FangJet. The weather services receive it only to answer the request; their own privacy policies describe how long they keep request logs.

When you view your current location, the app also sends its coordinates to your phone's own place lookup to find the name of the town, such as "Knoxville": Apple's on iPhone and iPad, and Google's on Android. The name is kept on your device, and the app asks again only after you have moved about a kilometre (just over half a mile). Cities you add yourself already have their names, so they are not looked up.

Usage statistics

The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase to count how its features are used, so we can improve it. It records four events: finishing onboarding, adding a city, opening Settings, and a weather request failing (with the type of error, such as "offline"). Firebase also records standard app events such as app opens, sessions and updates.

With these, Firebase receives your device model, operating system version, app version, language, and an approximate region worked out from your IP address. Events are tied to a random identifier that Firebase creates for each installation of the app. They are not linked to your name, email, contacts or any account, and the app does not use your device's advertising identifier.

Google keeps this data for 14 months. On iPhone and iPad in the European Union, the rest of the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, usage statistics stay off until you turn them on. Wherever you are, you can turn them on or off at any time:

  • iPhone and iPad: Settings, then Privacy, then Share usage statistics.
  • Android: Settings, then About, then Share usage statistics.

When it is off, the app stops sending analytics events, including Firebase's automatic ones.

Remote configuration

The app downloads settings from Firebase Remote Config, which lets us switch features such as the radar on or off and adjust the walk-time advice without an app update. To do this, Firebase receives your device's country and language codes, time zone, operating system version and app identifiers.

Crash reports (Android)

If the Android app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics sends a crash report containing the technical details of the error, your device model and operating system version, so we can fix the problem. Crash reports do not include your location or saved cities and are not linked to your identity.

Third-party services

The app talks to these services directly from your device. Like any website, each one also receives your IP address when the app contacts it.

  • OpenWeather: city search, and current conditions and forecasts when Open-Meteo can't provide them. OpenWeather privacy policy
  • Open-Meteo: current conditions, hourly and 7-day forecasts, and air quality. Open-Meteo terms and privacy
  • RainViewer: radar images for the map area around your city. RainViewer privacy policy
  • OpenStreetMap: the base map under the radar, for the same map area. OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy
  • Apple (iPhone and iPad): the name of your current location, from Apple's place lookup. Apple privacy policy
  • Google (Android): the name of your current location, from Android's place lookup, which Google Play services provides. Google privacy policy
  • Google Firebase: usage statistics, remote configuration and, on Android, crash reports. Privacy and security in Firebase

Children

Sunny & Misty is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Your choices and rights

  • You can deny or revoke location and notification access in your device's settings at any time.
  • You can turn off usage statistics in the app's Settings.
  • Deleting the app removes all data it stored on your device.

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access or delete personal data held about you. Because the analytics data is not linked to your identity, we usually cannot find a specific person's data, but contact us and we will help where we can.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the app handles your data, we will update this page and the date at the top.
