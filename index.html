Best walk times

Sunny picks the time, and a second one if you're busy

Sunny looks through the rest of the day, or after dark through tomorrow, and finds the spells that are good for a walk: dry, comfortable, in daylight, with clean enough air and pavement that's safe for paws. When is the best time to walk a dog?

  • When, and for how long. "2 to 4 PM", not just a single hour, so you can fit it around your day.
  • A second option. Can't make it then? "Or 6 to 8 PM."
  • Why it's later. "The rain's gone by then." "Cooler by then." Or, on the worst days, "Paw check: about 139° now. Dangerously hot."
  • Potty breaks only. When storms, gales, smoke or extreme heat or cold make a proper walk a bad idea, Sunny says so.

Meet the forecasters

Two pets, one sky. Sun, cloud, rain, storms, snow, fog, heat, cold and night each get their own scene, with a fresh caption every day.

Sunny

A golden retriever who loves a walk, as long as it won't mean soggy or scorched paws. Sunny puts on a scarf in the cold and sunglasses in the heat, and never says no to snow zoomies.

Misty

A grey-and-white tuxedo cat who watches the weather from the window. Misty keeps an eye on the clouds, the rain and the moon, hides under the bed when it thunders, and occasionally knocks the Wi-Fi off the shelf.

On the right days the pets dress up too: bats and a witch's hat for Halloween, autumn leaves and a pie for Thanksgiving.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. There are no ads, no subscriptions and no account to make.

Which phones does it run on?

iPhone and iPad with iOS 17 or later, and Android phones. It's on the App Store and Google Play.

How does Sunny choose the walk times?

Sunny checks each hour left today (after dark, tomorrow's) for rain, how warm or cold it feels, daylight, wind, storms, air quality and how hot the pavement is. The first good spell is the best time, and the next one is a second option. With no good hours left, the card says why. If you don't have a dog, switch off "I have a dog" in Settings to hide the walk card.

Why is the pavement so much hotter than the air?

Asphalt soaks up the sun. On a sunny 25°C (77°F) day it can pass 50°C (122°F), hot enough to hurt paws. Paw Check estimates it from the temperature and the sun. If in doubt, hold the back of your hand on the pavement for seven seconds: too hot for your hand is too hot for paws. More in our guide to paw safety.

Do I need a dog?

Not at all. Cat people, and people with no pets at all, get the same forecast and the same two pets. Without a dog the walk card and the walk nudge are hidden.

Where does the weather come from?

Forecasts, current conditions and air quality come from Open-Meteo, with OpenWeather as a backup, and the radar from RainViewer.

What does it know about me?

Very little, and nothing that identifies you. Your cities and settings stay on your phone. The privacy policy has the details.
